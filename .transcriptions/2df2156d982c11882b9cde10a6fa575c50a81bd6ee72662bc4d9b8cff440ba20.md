# Degrees of a vertex

- Let $G = (V, A)$ be a directed graph, where $V$ is the set of vertices and $A$ is the set of arcs. For any vertex $v \in V$, we define:
  - The **out-degree** of $v$: $d^+(v)$, which is the number of arcs leaving $v$.
  - The **in-degree** of $v$: $d^-(v)$, which is the number of arcs entering $v$.

Then, we have:

$$\sum_{v \in V} d^+(v) = \sum_{v \in V} d^-(v) = |A|$$

where $|A|$ is the total number of arcs in the graph.

- Interpretation: each outgoing arc from a vertex contributes to that vertex's out-degree, and each incoming arc contributes to the in-degree of the vertex it points to. Thus, each arc is counted once in the sum of out-degrees and once in the sum of in-degrees.
