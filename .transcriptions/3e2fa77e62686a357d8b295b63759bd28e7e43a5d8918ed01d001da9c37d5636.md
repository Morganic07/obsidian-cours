The next theorem shows that the decomposition $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2$ in Example 1 can be computed without having an orthogonal basis for $\mathbb{R}^n$. It is enough to have an orthogonal basis only for $W$.

**The Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem**

Let $W$ be a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Then each $\mathbf{y}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ can be written uniquely in the form

$\mathbf{y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \mathbf{z} \qquad (1)$

where $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is in $W$ and $\mathbf{z}$ is in $W^\perp$. In fact, if $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_p\}$ is any orthogonal basis of $W$, then

$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}{\mathbf{u}_1 \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}\mathbf{u}_1 + \cdots + \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_p}{\mathbf{u}_p \cdot \mathbf{u}_p}\mathbf{u}_p \qquad (2)$

and $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{y}}$.
