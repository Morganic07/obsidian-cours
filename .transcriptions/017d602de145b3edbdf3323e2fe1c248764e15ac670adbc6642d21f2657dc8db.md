**Theorem**

For given power series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n(x-\alpha)^n$, there are only three possibilities:

1. The series converges only when $x = \alpha$.
2. The series converges for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
3. There exists a positive number $R$ ($0 < R < +\infty$) such that the series converges absolutely if $|x-\alpha| < R$ and the series diverges if $|x-\alpha| > R$. Anything can happen at the particular cases $x-\alpha = R$ and $x-\alpha = -R$.
