The following theorem shows that uniform convergence allows limits switching.

**Theorem**

Let $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of functions $f_n : X \to \mathbb{R}$ and $a$ a limit point of $X$. Assume that

- $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ \exists \ell_n \in \mathbb{R},\ \lim_{x\to a} f_n(x) = \ell_n$
- The sequence $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly on $X$ to a function $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$

Then the sequence $(\ell_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is convergent to a limit $\ell$ and we have

$$\ell = \lim_{x\to a} f(x)$$

that is

$$\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left(\lim_{x\to a} f_n(x)\right) = \lim_{x\to a}\left(\lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n(x)\right)$$
