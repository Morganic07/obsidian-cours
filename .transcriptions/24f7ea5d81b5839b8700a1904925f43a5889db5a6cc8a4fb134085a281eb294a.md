**Regular Graph**

Let $k$ be a natural number. A graph $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ is said to be *$k$-regular* if and only if every vertex of $G$ has degree $k$:

$$\forall v \in V,\ d(v) = k.$$

$G$ is said to be *regular* if and only if there exists a natural number $k$ such that $G$ is $k$-regular.

**Proposition**

Let $k$ be a natural number. If $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ is a $k$-regular graph, then $k \cdot \text{card}(V) = 2 \cdot \text{card}(E)$.
