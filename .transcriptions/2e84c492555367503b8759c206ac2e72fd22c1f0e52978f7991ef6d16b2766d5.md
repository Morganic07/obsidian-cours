**The Chi-Squared Distribution**

**DEFINITION** For a positive integer $\nu$, let $Z_1, \ldots, Z_\nu$ be iid standard normal random variables. Then the **chi-squared distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom (df)** is defined to be the distribution of the rv

$$X = Z_1^2 + \cdots + Z_\nu^2$$

This will sometimes be denoted by $X \sim \chi^2_\nu$.

**PROPOSITION** The chi-squared distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom is the gamma distribution with $\alpha = \nu/2$ and $\beta = 2$. In particular, the pdf of the $\chi^2_\nu$ distribution is

$$f(x;\nu) = \frac{1}{2^{\nu/2}\Gamma(\nu/2)} x^{(\nu/2)-1} e^{-x/2} \qquad x > 0$$

**PROPOSITION**
1. If $X_3 = X_1 + X_2$, $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent, $X_1 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_1}$, and $X_2 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_2}$, then $X_3 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_1+\nu_2}$.
2. If $X_3 = X_1 + X_2$, $X_1$ and $X_2$ are independent, $X_1 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_1}$, and $X_3 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_3}$ with $\nu_3 > \nu_1$, then $X_2 \sim \chi^2_{\nu_3-\nu_1}$.

The chi-squared distribution is *additive*

The chi-squared distribution is *substractive*
