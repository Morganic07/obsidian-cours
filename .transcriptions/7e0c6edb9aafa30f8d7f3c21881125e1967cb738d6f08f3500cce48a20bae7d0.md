**Infinite solutions** : $2x + 4y = 6$, $4x + 8y = 12$, soit $\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 4 & 8 & 12 \end{array}\right]$

- Row picture : equations describe the identical line. Infinite amount of answers that satisfy both equations.
- Column picture, $x\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} + y\begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix}$ : vectors are colinear (on top of eachother). Linear combination has span of only one line. Answer lies within span, and unlimited combinations of $x$ and $y$ can combine to make $(6, 12)$.

**No solutions** : $2x + 4y = 6$, $4x + 8y = 13$, soit $\left[\begin{array}{cc|c} 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 4 & 8 & 13 \end{array}\right]$

- Row picture : equations describe the parallel lines. No answers that satisfy both equations.
- Column picture, $x\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} + y\begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 13 \end{bmatrix}$ : vectors are colinear and have a 1D span of just one line. Answer does not lie within span, so no combination of vectors can combine to form $(6, 13)$.
