Let $S_n = \sum_{n=0}^{n} a_k$, $T_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} b_k$, $S = \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n$ and $T = \lim_{n\to+\infty} T_n$. The *n*th partial sum of the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} (a_n + \lambda b_n)$ is given by

$$U_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (a_k + \lambda b_k) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k + \lambda \sum_{k=0}^{n} b_k = S_n + \lambda T_n$$

since the sequence $(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ and $(T_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ are convergent, we have

$$\lim_{n\to+\infty} U_n = \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n + \lambda \lim_{n\to+\infty} T_n = S + \lambda T$$

This shows that the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} (a_n + \lambda b_n)$ is convergent and that its sum is $S + \lambda T$.
