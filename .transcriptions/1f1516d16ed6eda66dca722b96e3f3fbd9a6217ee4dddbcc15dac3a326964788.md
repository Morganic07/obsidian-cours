The normal distribution serves as a reasonable approximation to the binomial pmf when $n$ is large because the binomial distribution is *additive*; i.e., a binomial rv can be expressed as the sum of other, iid rvs. Other additive distributions include the Poisson, negative binomial, gamma, and (of course) normal distributions;

In particular, CLT justifies normal approximations to the following distributions:

- Poisson, when $\mu$ is large
- Negative binomial, when $r$ is large
- Gamma, when $\alpha$ is large
