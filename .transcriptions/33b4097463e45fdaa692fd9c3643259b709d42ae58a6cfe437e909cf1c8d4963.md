Question: If $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + \dots + x_n$ follows a Student's t-distribution with $(n-1)$ degrees of freedom, what can we say about the **sum of the squares** of these values?

In other words, what is the distribution of → $x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2$?

**Answer:**
Statisticians have shown that **if *X* is normal**, then the sum of squares of $n$ values of $x_i$, namely,

$x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2$

has a $\boldsymbol{\chi^2}$ **distribution** with $(\boldsymbol{n} - \mathbf{1})$ **degrees of freedom**.
