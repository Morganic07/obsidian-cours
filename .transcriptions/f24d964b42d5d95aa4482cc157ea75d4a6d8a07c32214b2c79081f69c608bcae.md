**Remark**

These inequalities give a more accurate estimation to the sum of the series than the *n*th partial sum does.

**Example**

We use the previous proposition to obtain an estimate of $\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} \frac{1}{n^3}$.
If we take for example $n = 20$, we have

$$S_{20} + \int_{21}^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^3}\,dx \leq S \leq S_{20} + \int_{20}^{+\infty} \frac{1}{x^3}\,dx$$

This gives

$$S_{20} + \frac{1}{2(21)^2} \leq S \leq S_{20} + \frac{1}{2(20)^2}$$

We have $S_{20} = 1.20087$. This gives $1.20197 \leq S \leq 1.20212$.
