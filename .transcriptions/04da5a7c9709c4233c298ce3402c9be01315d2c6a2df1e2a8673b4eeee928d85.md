The entry $s_{ij}$ in $S$ for $i \neq j$ is called the **covariance** of $x_i$ and $x_j$. Observe that in Example 3, the covariance between $x_1$ and $x_3$ is 0 because the $(1,3)$-entry in $S$ is 0. Statisticians say that $x_1$ and $x_3$ are **uncorrelated**. Analysis of the multivariate data in $\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_N$ is greatly simplified when most or all of the variables $x_1, \ldots, x_p$ are uncorrelated, that is, when the covariance matrix of $\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_N$ is diagonal or nearly diagonal.
