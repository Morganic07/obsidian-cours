❶ Since $p > 1$, the $p$-series $\sum_{n\geq 1} \frac{1}{n^p}$ is convergent. Assume that $\lim_{n\to+\infty} n^p a_n = 0$. Then $a_n = o\left(\frac{1}{n^p}\right)$ and $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ converges.

❷ Since $p \leq 1$, the $p$-series $\sum_{n\geq 1} \frac{1}{n^p}$ is divergent. Since $\lim_{n\to+\infty} n^p a_n = +\infty$, there exists $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\forall n \geq n_0,\ n^p a_n \geq 1$. Therefore $\forall n \geq n_0,\ a_n \geq \frac{1}{n^p}$. Then the comparison test shows that $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ diverges.
