**Theorem**

Let $\sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(x-\alpha)^n$ be a power series with radius of convergence $R > 0$. The series of functions is normally convergent on each interval $[\alpha - r, \alpha + r]$ with $0 < r < R$ which leads to the continuity of the sum $S$ on $]\alpha - R, \alpha + R[$.
Moreover, if the series $\sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(\alpha - R - \alpha)^n = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(-R)^n$ is convergent, then the sum $S$ is continuous at $\alpha - R$ and if the series $\sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(\alpha + R - \alpha)^n = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n R^n$ is convergent, then the sum $S$ is continuous at $\alpha + R$.

**Corollary**

With the conditions of the theorem, if if the series $\sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(\alpha - R - \alpha)^n = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(-R)^n$ is convergent, then $S(\alpha - R) = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(-R)^n$ and if the series $\sum_{n\geq 0} a_n(\alpha + R - \alpha)^n = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n R^n$ is convergent, then $S(\alpha + R) = \sum_{n\geq 0} a_n R^n$
