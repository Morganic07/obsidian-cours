**Definition**

A series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ is said to be absolutely convergent if the series of absolute values $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} |a_n|$ is convergent.

1. The series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ is absolutely convergent, since the $p$-series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \frac{1}{n^2}$ is convergent.
2. The alternating harmonic series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ is convergent thanks to the alternating test, but it is not absolutely convergent since the harmonic series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \frac{1}{n}$ is divergent.

For series with positive terms, absolute convergence is the same as convergence.
