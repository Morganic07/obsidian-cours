❑ A given study may produce measurements that are:
- o **paired** or
- o totally **independent**.

❑ The statistical tests should be chosen accordingly.

For example, we are interested in studying the effect of a medical treatment on insulin rate. Here are two possible experimental designs that would help answer this question:

➢ Insulin rate is measured on 30 patients **before and after** the medical treatment. <u>Data are thus organized by</u> **pairs** (every patient is associated to two measurements).
→ An appropriate test to use here would be a **two-sample t-test**.

➢ Insulin rate is measured on 30 patients receiving a placebo and 30 **other** patients receiving the medical treatment. In this case, all of the measurements are **independent**.
→ An appropriate test to use here would be an **independent** two-group t-test.

À vérifier : le texte dit « two-sample t-test » pour des données appariées (before/after), ce qui semble faux (on attendrait un test t apparié), mais je l'ai recopié tel quel.
