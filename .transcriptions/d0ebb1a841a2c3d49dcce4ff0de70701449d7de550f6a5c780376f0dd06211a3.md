Simple Chain

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. A chain $C = (v_0, e_1, v_1, \ldots, e_p, v_p)$ in $G$ is said to be simple if and only if the edges constituting $C$ are pairwise distinct:

$$\forall (i,j) \in \{1, \ldots, p\}^2, (i \neq j) \implies e_i \neq e_j$$

Elementary Chain

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. A chain $C = (v_0, e_1, v_1, \ldots, e_p, v_p)$ in $G$ is said to be elementary if and only if the vertices constituting $C$ are pairwise distinct:

$$\forall (i,j) \in \{0, \ldots, p\}^2, (i \neq j) \implies v_i \neq v_j$$

Cycle

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. A cycle in $G$ is a simple chain $C = (v_0, e_1, v_1, \ldots, e_p, v_p)$ such that $\ell(C) > 0$ and $v_p = v_0$.
