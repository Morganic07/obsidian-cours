If $\mathbf{x}$ represents a variable vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, then a **change of variable** is an equation of the form

$\mathbf{x} = P\mathbf{y}$, or equivalently, $\mathbf{y} = P^{-1}\mathbf{x} \qquad (1)$

where $P$ is an invertible matrix and $\mathbf{y}$ is a new variable vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Here $\mathbf{y}$ is the coordinate vector of $\mathbf{x}$ relative to the basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ determined by the columns of $P$.

If the change of variable (1) is made in a quadratic form $\mathbf{x}^TA\mathbf{x}$, then

$\mathbf{x}^TA\mathbf{x} = (P\mathbf{y})^TA(P\mathbf{y}) = \mathbf{y}^TP^TAP\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{y}^T(P^TAP)\mathbf{y} \qquad (2)$ (a new quadratic form)

and the new matrix of the quadratic form is $P^TAP$. Since $A$ is symmetric, Theorem 2 guarantees that there is an *orthogonal* matrix $P$ such that $P^TAP$ is a diagonal matrix $D$, and the quadratic form in (2) becomes $\mathbf{y}^TD\mathbf{y}$. This is the strategy of the next example.

Annotations : orthogonal → $P^TP = I$ ; $A = PDP^T = PDP^{-1}$

$A = PDP^T \Leftrightarrow P^TA = P^TPDP^T \Leftrightarrow P^TA = DP^T \Leftrightarrow P^TAP = DP^TP \Leftrightarrow P^TAP = D$
