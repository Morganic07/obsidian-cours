**Theorem**

Suppose that $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a sequence of functions of class $\mathcal{C}^1$ defined from $I$ to $\mathbb{R}$ such that

1. There exists $x_0 \in I$ such that the sequence $(f_n(x_0))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is convergent,
2. The sequence $(f_n')_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly on $I$ to a function $g : I \to \mathbb{R}$.

Then $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly on $I$ to a function of class $\mathcal{C}^1$, $f$, on $I$ and $f' = g$. This means $(\lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n)' = \lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n'$.
