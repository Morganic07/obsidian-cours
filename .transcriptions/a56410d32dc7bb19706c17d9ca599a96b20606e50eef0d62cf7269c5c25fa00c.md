If we consider a finite population of size $N$, then the population mean is given by $\mu$, and the variance is given by

$$\mu = \sum_{i=1,\dots,N} \frac{x_i}{N}$$

$$\sigma^2 = \sum_{i=1,\dots,N} \frac{(x_i - \mu)^2}{N}$$

If we consider an infinite population, we assume there is a probability distribution associated the values of the variable(s) we are interested in. The variable is a r.v. with that distribution, its mean is the expectation of the rv, and its variance, the variance of the rv as seen in the course of Probability.
