Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix. The matrix $A$ produces a linear transformation from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$. What actually happens in $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ can be expressed in terms of **4 subspaces** (two of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and two of $\mathbb{R}^m$).

The **four fundamental subspaces** are:

- The **Column Space** Col $A$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$ — in some books → $C(A)$
- The **Row Space** Col $A^T$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ — in some books → $C(A^T)$
- The **Null Space** Nul $A$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ — in some books → $N(A)$
- The **Left Null Space** Nul $A^T$, a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$ — in some books → $N(A^T)$

Encadré :

- The **column space** of a matrix is the **image** or **range** of the corresponding matrix transformation.
- Recall Row $A$ = Col $A^T$ which means Row $A^T$ = Col $A$
