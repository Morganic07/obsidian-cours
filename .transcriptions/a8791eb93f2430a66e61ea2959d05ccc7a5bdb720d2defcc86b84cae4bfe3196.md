**DEFINITION** Let $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be a random sample from a distribution that depends on a parameter $\theta$. Then an estimator $\hat{\theta}$ is a **consistent** estimator of $\theta$ if $\hat{\theta}$ converges to $\theta$ as $n \to \infty$, either in the sense that

1. $E[(\hat{\theta} - \theta)^2] \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, or
2. $P(|\hat{\theta} - \theta| \geq \varepsilon) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.$^1$

---

$^1$In fact, Statement 1 implies Statement 2. But there exist unusual cases for which Statement 1 fails—typically, when the variance of the estimator is infinite—and Statement 2 still holds.
