Theorem

Suppose that $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is a sequence of differentiable functions defined from an interval $I \subset \mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$ such that

1. There exists $x_0 \in I$ such that the sequence $(f_n(x_0))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is convergent,
2. The sequence $(f_n')_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly on $I$ to a function $g : I \to \mathbb{R}$.

Then $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly on $I$ to a differentiable function $f$ and $f' = g$. This means $(\lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n)' = \lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n'$.
