**Theorem**

Let $a < b$ be two real numbers, $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ a sequence of functions defined on the interval $[a,b]$. Assume that

1. $\forall n \in \mathbb{N}$, $f_n$ is continuous on $[a,b]$.
2. The series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} (f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges uniformly $[a,b]$.

Then the function $S = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} f_n$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \left(\int_a^b f_n(t)\,dt\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges to $\int_a^b S(t)\,dt$, that is

$$\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \int_a^b f_n(t)\,dt = \int_a^b \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} f_n(t)\,dt$$

À vérifier : dans l'hypothèse 2, « converges uniformly [a, b] » semble sans « on » devant $[a,b]$, comme dans l'image.
