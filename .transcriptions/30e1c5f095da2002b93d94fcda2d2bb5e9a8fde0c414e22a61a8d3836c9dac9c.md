**Proposition**

Let $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ be a series with positive real terms.

1. If the series is convergent and its sum is $S$, then $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ S_n \leq S$.
2. If the series is divergent, then $\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n = +\infty$.

**Proof.**

1. We have $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ R_n = S - S_n = \sum_{k\geq n+1} a_k \geq 0$ since we have a series with positive terms. This implies that $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ S_n \leq S$.
2. Since the series diverges, the increasing sequence $(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is not bounded above. Thus $\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n = +\infty$.
