Suppose you have ten measurements:
2.1, 2.4, 2.2, 2.3, 2.7, .2.5, 2.4, 2.6, 2.6, 2.9
We want to visualize its relative frequency histogram, to see how the measurements are **distributed**.

1) We divide the axis of measurements into intervals of equal width (here, 0.2 units starting at 2.05)

2) We put a rectangle on each interval such that the height is proportional to the *frequency* or *relative frequency* of the total number of measurements that fall in that interval

Class intervals or classes: each observation is contained in exactly one class

Figure : Histogramme de fréquence relative. L'axe vertical (Relative Frequency) est gradué de 0 à .3 (repères 0, .1, .2, .3) et l'axe horizontal (Axis of Measurement) porte les repères 2.05, 2.25, 2.45, 2.65, 2.85, 3.05. Il y a cinq rectangles : le premier est de hauteur environ .2, les deuxième et troisième sont les plus hauts (environ .3), les deux derniers sont de hauteur environ .1.
