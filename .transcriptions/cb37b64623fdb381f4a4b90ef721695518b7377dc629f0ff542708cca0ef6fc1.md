$$A = \begin{bmatrix} x_{11} & x_{12} & & x_{1n} \\ & & \ddots & \\ x_{m1} & & & x_{mn} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} u_{11} & & u_{m1} \\ & \ddots & \\ u_{1m} & & u_{mm} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_1 & & & 0 \\ & \ddots & & \\ & & \sigma_r & \\ 0 & & & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_{11} & & v_{1n} \\ & \ddots & \\ v_{n1} & & v_{nn} \end{bmatrix}$$

Au-dessus des matrices : A, U, S, $V^T$. Dimensions : A est $m \times n$, U est $m \times m$, S est $m \times n$, $V^T$ est $n \times n$.

Encadré rouge : $A = \sigma_1 u_1 v_1^T + \dots + \sigma_r u_r v_r^T$

Encadré orange : **V** and **U** → orthogonal.

**V** → is also called the **loadings** matrix

$v_{ij}$ → weights for each original variable when calculating the PC.
**How much each variable contributes to a particular PC.**
**Large loadings** (+ or −) indicate that a particular variable has a **strong relationship to a particular PC**.
The **sign** of a loading indicates whether a variable and a PC are **positively** or **negatively correlated**.

**U** → is also called the **scores** matrix.

$u_{ij}$ → the original data in a **rotated coordinate system**.
