**Definition**

Let $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of functions defined on a set $X \subset \mathbb{R}$ and $(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be the sequence of the partial sums. We say that the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} f_n$ converges pointwise on $X$ if the sequence of functions $(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges pointwise on $X$. In that case, the pointwise limit of the sequence $(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is called the sum of the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} f_n$ and is denoted by $\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} f_n$. We have

$$\forall x \in X,\ S(x) = \left(\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} f_n\right)(x) = \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n(x) = \lim_{n\to+\infty} \sum_{k=0}^{n} f_k(x)$$

If the series of functions $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is not pointwise convergent, we say that it is divergent.

À vérifier : dans la dernière phrase, le signe $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ n'est suivi d'aucun terme $f_n$, tel quel dans l'image.
