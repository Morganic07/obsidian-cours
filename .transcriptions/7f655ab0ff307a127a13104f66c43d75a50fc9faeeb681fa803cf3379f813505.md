Definition

Let $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequences of functions defined from a non-empty subset $X$ of $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$. We say that the sequence $(f_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges pointwise on $X$ if for any $x \in X$, the sequences $(f_n(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ converges on $X$. This allows to obtain a function $f$ defined on $X$ by

$$\forall x \in X,\ f(x) = \lim_{n\to+\infty} f_n(x)$$

The function $f$ is called the pointwise limit of the sequence $f_n$. Equivalently, we have

$$\forall x \in X,\ \forall \epsilon > 0,\ \exists N \in \mathbb{N},\ (n \geq N \Rightarrow |f_n(x) - f(x)| \leq \epsilon)$$

Remark

We notice that $N$ depends on $x$ and $\epsilon$.
