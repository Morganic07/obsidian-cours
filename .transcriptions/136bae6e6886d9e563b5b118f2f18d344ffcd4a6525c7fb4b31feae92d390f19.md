❶ The previous theorem says that the radius of convergence remains the same when a power series is differentiated or integrated. However, this does not mean that the interval of convergence will remain the same. The properties at the endpoint can be different. It may happen that the series converges at an endpoint while its differentiated series will not be convergent.

❷ Here are the basic facts on power series. To simplify the notation, we consider power series centered at 0. Every series has a radius of convergence $0 \le R \le +\infty$. The power series converges absolutely for $|x| < R$ and diverges if $|x| > R$. The convergence is normal (and therefore uniform) on every interval $|x| < r$ where $0 \le r < R$. If $R > 0$, then the sum of the power series is indefinitely differentiable in $|x| < R$ and its derivatives are obtain by differentiating the original series term-by-term.
