1. If $r = 1$, then $S_n = a(n+1)$ which tends to $\pm\infty$ to $n \to +\infty$. The series is divergent.

2. If $r \neq 1$, then we have:

$$S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} ar^k \quad \text{and} \quad rS_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} ar^{k+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} ar^k$$

We obtain: $S_n - rS_n = a - ar^{n+1}$. This gives $S_n = a\dfrac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}$.

Again, we have several cases:

1. If $-1 < r < 1$, then $\lim_{n\to+\infty} r^n = 0$. Shows that the series is convergent and its sum is given by

$$\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n = \lim_{n\to+\infty} a\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r} = \frac{a}{1-r}$$

2. If $r > 1$ or $r \leq -1$ the sequence $r^n$ diverges and $\lim_{n\to\infty} S_n$ does not exists. Thus the series diverges.
