- If $\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ is convergent, then inequality (1) gives

$$S_n - a_1 \leq \int_1^n f(x)\,dx \leq \int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$$

since $f(x) \geq 0$. Therefore

$$\forall n \geq 1,\ S_n \leq a_1 + \int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$$

This shows that the sequence $(S_n)_{n\geq 1}$ is bounded above and by Property 1, the series $\sum_{n\geq 1} a_n$ is convergent.

- If $\int_1^{+\infty} f(x)\,dx$ is divergent, then $\lim_{n\to+\infty} \int_1^n f(x)\,dx = +\infty$ since $f(x) \geq 0$. Using inequality (2), we obtain

$$\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_{n-1} \geq \lim_{n\to+\infty} \int_1^n f(x)\,dx$$

Therefore $\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n = +\infty$ and the series $\sum_{n\geq 1} a_n$ is divergent.
