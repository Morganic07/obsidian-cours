Given a subset $\mathcal{A}$ of $\mathcal{P}(\Omega)$, we say $\mathcal{A}$ is a **sigma-algebra** of sets if it satisfies the following properties:

i. $\Omega \in \mathcal{A}$,

ii. if $B \in \mathcal{A}$, then $B^c \in \mathcal{A}$ (that is, $\mathcal{A}$ is stable under complements)

iii. if $(B_i)_i$, $i = 1,2,3 \dots$ is a sequence of elements in $\mathcal{A}$, then $\bigcup_i B_i \in \mathcal{A}$ (that is, $\mathcal{A}$ is stable under countable unions)
