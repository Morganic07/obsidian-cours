**Theorem**

Let $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ be a series. If the series is convergent, then $\lim_{n\to+\infty} a_n = 0$.

**Proof.**
Let $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k$. Then we have: $S_n - S_{n-1} = a_n$. Since the series is convergent, we have: $\lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n = \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_{n-1} = S$. This implies that $\lim_{n\to+\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_n - \lim_{n\to+\infty} S_{n-1} = 0$.

**Remark**

The previous theorem gives only a necessary condition. This is not a sufficient condition as shown by the harmonic series
