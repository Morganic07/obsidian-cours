This formula comes from the fact that the normal distribution can be used to approximate the binomial distribution if $np$ and $n(1-p) \geq 5$.

$$\left\{\begin{aligned}
&\left.\begin{aligned}\mu &= np \\ \sigma &= \sqrt{np(1-p)}\end{aligned}\right\} \textit{for a binomial} \\
&Z = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} = \frac{x-np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}} = \frac{\frac{x}{n} - p}{\frac{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}{n}} = \frac{P_s - P}{\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}}
\end{aligned}\right.$$

As $n$ gets large and $p \to .5$, then the binomial→normal. Use when $np$ and $n(1-p) > 5$.
