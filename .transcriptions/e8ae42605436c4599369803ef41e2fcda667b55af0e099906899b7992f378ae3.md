**Theorem**

Let $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ such that there exists $n_0$, with $a_n \neq 0$ for $n \geq n_0$.

1. If $\lim_{n\to+\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = L < 1$, then the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ is absolutely convergent.
2. If $\lim_{n\to+\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = L > 1$, or $\lim_{n\to+\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = +\infty$, then the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ is divergent.
3. If $\lim_{n\to+\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = 1$, no conclusion can be drawn about the convergence or the divergence of the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$.
