Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix whose distinct eigenvalues are $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_p$.

- a. For $1 \le k \le p$, the dimension of the eigenspace for $\lambda_k$ is less than or equal to the multiplicity of the eigenvalue $\lambda_k$.
- b. The matrix $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if the sum of the dimensions of the eigenspaces equals $n$, and this happens if and only if (*i*) the characteristic polynomial factors completely into linear factors and (*ii*) the dimension of the eigenspace for each $\lambda_k$ equals the multiplicity of $\lambda_k$.
- c. If $A$ is diagonalizable and $\mathcal{B}_k$ is a basis for the eigenspace corresponding to $\lambda_k$ for each $k$, then the total collection of vectors in the sets $\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_p$ forms an eigenvector basis for $\mathbb{R}^n$.
