We have $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$, $e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$. In particular, if we choose $x = 1$, we have $e = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \frac{1}{n!}$. We want to find an approximation of $e$. Here, we will use the Lagrange's form of the remainder term.

There exists $c$ between 0 and 1 such that $R_n(1) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}$. All the derivatives of the exponential function are equal to the exponential function. Therefore,

$$0 \leq R_n(1) = \frac{e}{(n+1)!} \leq \frac{3}{(n+1)!}$$

In order to have an accuracy of $10^{-5}$, it is enough to choose $n = 8$. We can see that the approximation is more efficient in this example than in the previous one.

À vérifier : l'image ne montre pas $e^c$ au numérateur de $R_n(1)$ (elle indique $f^{(n+1)}(c)$), et le passage à $\frac{e}{(n+1)!}$ est recopié tel qu'il apparaît.
