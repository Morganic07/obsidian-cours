**Proof.**

Since $c > 0$, there exits two strictly positive real numbers $m$ and $M$ such that $0 < m < c < M$. Moreover, $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = c$. Thus, there exists $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

$$\forall n \geq N,\; m < \frac{a_n}{b_n} < M$$

This implies

$$\forall n \geq N,\; m b_n < a_n < M b_n$$

If $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} b_n$ converges, then $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} M b_n$ converges and by the previous proposition, $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ converges. If $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} b_n$ diverges, then $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} m b_n$ diverges and by the comparison test, $\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}} a_n$ diverges.
