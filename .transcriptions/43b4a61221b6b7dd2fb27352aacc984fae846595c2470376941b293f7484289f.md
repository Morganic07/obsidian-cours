Bipartite Graph

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. $G$ is said to be bipartite if and only if there exists a partition $(V_1, V_2)$ of $V$ such that $V_1$ and $V_2$ are non-empty and:

$$\forall e \in E,\ e \text{ has one endpoint in } V_1 \text{ and the other in } V_2$$

Then we denote $G = (V_1, V_2, E, \gamma)$.

---

Complete Bipartite Graph

Let $G = (V_1, V_2, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected bipartite graph. $G$ is said to be complete if and only if it is simple and every vertex in $V_1$ is adjacent to every vertex in $V_2$.

---

Characterization of Bipartite Graphs

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. Then $G$ is bipartite if and only if every cycle in $G$ has even length.
