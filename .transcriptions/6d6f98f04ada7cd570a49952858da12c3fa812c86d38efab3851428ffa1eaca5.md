The multiplication rule is very useful when an experiment consists of several consecutive stages.

Define B the outcome of the first stage, and A the outcome of the second stage.

This cna be generalized for more stages, for example, $A_1$ is the outcome of a first stage, $A_2$ the outcome of a second stage, and $A_3$ the outcome of a third stage. Then

$$P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap A_3) = P(A_3 | A_1 \cap A_2) \cdot P(A_1 \cap A_2)$$
$$= P(A_3 | A_1 \cap A_2) \cdot P(A_2 | A_1) \cdot P(A_1)$$
