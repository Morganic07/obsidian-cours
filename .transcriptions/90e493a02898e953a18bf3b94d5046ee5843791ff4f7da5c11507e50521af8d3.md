It is easy to visualize the case in which $W = \mathbb{R}^2 = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\}$, with $\mathbf{u}_1$ and $\mathbf{u}_2$ orthogonal. Any $\mathbf{y}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ can be written in the form

$\mathbf{y} = \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}{\mathbf{u}_1 \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}\mathbf{u}_1 + \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_2}{\mathbf{u}_2 \cdot \mathbf{u}_2}\mathbf{u}_2 \qquad (3)$

Figure : $\mathbf{y}$ est la somme de $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_1$ = projection onto $\mathbf{u}_1$ et de $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_2$ = projection onto $\mathbf{u}_2$. A vector $\mathbf{y}$ decomposed into the sum of two projections.

The Theorem **decomposes** each $\mathbf{y}$ in $\text{Span}\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_p\}$ into the sum of "$p$" projections onto **one-dimensional subspaces** that are **mutually orthogonal**.
