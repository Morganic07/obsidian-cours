Adjacent Vertices

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. We say that $v_1 \in V$ and $v_2 \in V$ are neighbors or adjacent if and only if there exists an edge $e \in E$ whose endpoints are $v_1$ and $v_2$. If $v \in V$ is not adjacent to any other vertex, it is said to be isolated.

Loop

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. A loop is an edge whose two endpoints are the same, i.e., $e$ is a loop if and only if $\exists v \in V$ such that $\gamma(e) = (v, v)$.

Multiple Edges

Let $G = (V, E, \gamma)$ be an undirected graph. Two edges of $G$ are said to be multiple or parallel if and only if they share the same endpoints.
