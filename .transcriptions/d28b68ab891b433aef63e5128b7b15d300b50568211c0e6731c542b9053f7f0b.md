Let $A$, $\lambda_1$, and $\mathbf{u}_1$ be as in Theorem 6. Then the maximum value of $\mathbf{x}^TA\mathbf{x}$ subject to the constraints

$\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{x} = 1, \quad \mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{u}_1 = 0$

is the second greatest eigenvalue, $\lambda_2$, and this maximum is attained when $\mathbf{x}$ is an eigenvector $\mathbf{u}_2$ corresponding to $\lambda_2$.
